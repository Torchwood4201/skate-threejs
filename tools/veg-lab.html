THE VEGETATION LIBRARY — every species in src/world/vegetation/ and the sky volumes in src/world/sky/, built here and shot from where a chase camera sees them. The thing on trial is shots/ask/m2.png: flat dark-green cones on a hillside. Every frame below is the answer to that frame, so judge them at the distance they are shot from and not closer.
THE HILLSIDE — the frame that has to beat the ask. A channel floor, forested banks, eye at 1.6 m, everything under the live level-of-detail bands for the selected tier.
THE STAND — all five species together with a 1.75 m rider pole, from the SHADE side, where a silhouette has nothing but its own outline to sell it.
THE CONTACT SHEET — each species at four seeds, from the SUN side. Two seeds that look the same are a bug: a hillside is two hundred of these.
THE LEVELS — one tree meshed three ways, side by side at 12 m so the cuts are visible, then the same tree shot at the distance each level actually takes over. If the far card is a different tree from the near mesh, it shows here.
THE SHEETS — every pixel the forest is made of: one foliage atlas, one tiling bark map, and one far-distance card per species. If a canopy reads as cardboard, the alpha in the first of these is where to look — a spray whose outline is a rounded blob is a cone with extra steps.
THE SKY — the cloud volume over the map's OWN generated panorama (loaded through map 2's dressSky, so this is the real composition and not a mock-up of it), at three coverages. The middle one is the default.
THE NUMBERS — measured triangle counts per species per level, the live forest cost from the hillside camera, the band table for every tier, and what the clouds cost to build and to march. A stated budget nobody measured is a stated budget that is wrong.
